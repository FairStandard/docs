The Marketplace is where litigants go when they realize they need help. They arrive with a case type in mind — custody, divorce, a modification — and a jurisdiction, and they are looking for someone they can trust with both. A well-built Marketplace profile meets them at that moment: it tells them exactly what you handle, where you practice, and what working with you looks like. Done right, it turns a litigant's search into a consultation request without any outbound effort on your part.

## Creating Your Marketplace Listing

<Steps>
  <Step title="Go to Your Profile Settings">
    From your attorney dashboard, click your name in the top-right corner and select **Marketplace Profile**. This opens your listing editor.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set Your Practice Areas">
    Select every case type you handle. Options include Custody, Divorce, Child Support, Modifications, Protective Orders, and others. Litigants filter by case type first — the more precisely you match your actual practice, the better qualified your incoming inquiries will be.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set Your Jurisdictions">
    Add every state, and where applicable every county, where you are licensed and willing to take cases. Local rules vary by county courthouse, and litigants in those jurisdictions will specifically filter for attorneys who know their local court.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write Your Profile Bio">
    Write a short description of your practice, your approach, and your experience. This is the first thing a prospective client reads. Write it to the litigant, not to other attorneys — speak directly to the situation they are in and what you do about it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set Consultation Availability">
    Configure whether you are currently accepting new consultation requests and how you prefer to be contacted: in-platform message, email, or phone. Keeping this up to date prevents inquiries from going unanswered.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish">
    Save your profile. You are immediately discoverable in Marketplace searches that match your practice areas and jurisdictions — no approval delay.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What a Strong Profile Includes

A profile that consistently converts searches into consultations typically does the following:

- **Clear practice area focus** — List the case types you actually prioritize. A long list of every available option signals less credibility than a focused set that matches your real practice.
- **Specific jurisdictions** — Don't just add the state. Include the county courts where you regularly appear. Litigants facing a hearing in a specific courthouse will filter for local knowledge.
- **A bio written to the litigant** — Describe the situation your clients typically face and what you do to address it. Avoid generic credential lists. A litigant who reads your bio and thinks "that's my situation" is already halfway to calling you.
- **Responsive to consultation requests** — Profiles with faster average response times rank higher in search results. Set up notifications so you see incoming requests quickly.

<Tip>
  Litigants filter by jurisdiction and case type first. Make sure your jurisdictions are complete and up to date — missing a jurisdiction means missing clients from that area.
</Tip>

## For Legal Service Providers

Notaries, custody evaluators, mediators, and process servers follow the same listing process. When setting up your profile, select your provider type instead of practice areas — the options are tailored to service provider categories. Add your service area (the counties or regions you cover), and include any relevant credentials, certifications, or professional affiliations. Litigants and attorneys searching for a specific type of provider in a specific area will find your listing the same way they find attorneys.

<Note>
  Your Marketplace profile is separate from your case activity. Clients you acquire through the Marketplace connect to their cases just like any other client referral — through the standard case invitation flow.
</Note>
