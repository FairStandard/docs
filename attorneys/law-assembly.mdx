A single Texas custody case touches more than 7,000 statute sections spread across the Family Code, the Civil Practice and Remedies Code, the Government Code, and the Health and Safety Code — before you add the Texas Rules of Civil Procedure, local county rules, and the case law that controls how those statutes are actually applied. No attorney holds all of it. Assembling it manually case by case is slow, expensive, and unavoidably incomplete. Vero does it automatically: when a case is created, the engine identifies every applicable provision, organizes it by code and rule set, and surfaces it alongside the specific facts in that case where each provision is relevant.

## How law assembly works for attorneys

When a case is created with a jurisdiction and case type, the engine immediately begins identifying every applicable statute, procedural rule, and controlling precedent:

- **Jurisdiction-specific coverage** — Results are organized by code and rule set. A Texas custody matter surfaces the Texas Family Code, the Texas Rules of Civil Procedure, and the applicable local county rules as separate, browsable sections.
- **Fact-linked provisions** — Each statute section is connected to the specific facts and evidence in the case where it applies. You see not just what the law says, but why it matters to this client's situation.
- **Case law alongside statutes** — Controlling precedent and doctrine are surfaced in context with the statutes they interpret, so you see the full picture of how courts have applied the provision.
- **Procedural rules included** — Filing deadlines, evidentiary standards, and local standing orders are part of the assembled set, not a separate research task.

## Browsing the assembled law

The Assembled Law section appears in every case workspace. To navigate it:

- **Browse by code or category** — Use the left-panel index to move between code sections (e.g., Texas Family Code → Conservatorship) or filter by category such as Procedure, Evidence, or Jurisdiction.
- **Search by keyword** — The search bar returns full-text matches across all assembled provisions, with the case-specific relevance note shown in each result.
- **Review the full provision** — Each entry shows the complete text of the applicable statute or rule, the controlling precedent, and a note explaining why it is relevant to this specific case.
- **Pin, annotate, and cite** — Pin provisions you are actively working with, add your own annotations, and cite them directly into documents without copying and reformatting.

<Tip>
  Use the Assembled Law search to quickly locate the specific provision you need during drafting or in a hearing. Every provision links back to the evidence and filings in the case.
</Tip>

## Staying current

Statutory law and court rules change. The engine continuously monitors for updates and rule changes in the jurisdiction of each active case. When a statutory amendment or new local rule affects a provision that is part of your assembled set, you receive a notification identifying the changed provision and its impact on the case. You do not need to run a separate research check to know whether the law you relied on last month still reads the same way today.

<Note>
  Law assembly covers all 50 US states for family law case types. Coverage for additional practice areas is added on an ongoing basis.
</Note>
