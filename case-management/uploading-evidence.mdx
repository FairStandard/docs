The FairStandard evidence vault accepts every type of document, recording, and record you are likely to have in a family law case. Once you upload your files, the engine automatically categorizes, tags, and analyzes them — surfacing behavioral patterns, financial discrepancies, order violations, and gaps in discovery so you always know what you have and what it means.

## Supported File Types

| Category | Accepted Formats |
|---|---|
| Audio/Video Recordings | MP3, MP4, M4A, MOV |
| Text/Message Exports | TXT, CSV (iMessage, WhatsApp, SMS backup) |
| Court Documents & Filings | PDF, DOCX |
| Financial Records | PDF, XLSX (bank statements, tax returns, pay stubs) |
| Photos & Screenshots | PNG, JPG, HEIC |

## Uploading Your Files

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Your Case">
    Navigate to the case from your dashboard by clicking its name.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Go to the Evidence Vault">
    Click the **Evidence** tab in the case workspace to open your vault.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Upload Files">
    Drag and drop files onto the upload area, or click **Add Files** to browse your device. You can upload multiple files at once — there is no need to upload them one at a time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add Context (Optional)">
    After uploading, you can add a brief note to any file to describe what it contains or why it is relevant. This context helps the engine and anyone with shared access understand the significance of the document.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wait for Processing">
    FairStandard analyzes each file automatically after upload. A processing indicator shows progress for each file; most files are analyzed within a few minutes.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Upload everything you have — even files you think are not relevant. The engine is built to find signal in large, messy document sets. You can always remove files later if needed.
</Tip>

## How the Engine Organizes Your Evidence

Once a file is processed, FairStandard does more than store it. The engine automatically assigns each file to one or more categories, applies descriptive tags, and links it to relevant events on your case timeline and to the applicable statutes already assembled for your jurisdiction.

From there, the engine surfaces findings you might not find on your own: repeated patterns of behavior across communications, financial discrepancies between declared income and observed spending, documents referenced in filings but missing from discovery, and direct violations of existing court orders. Each finding is cited — tied to the specific file and the rule or statute it implicates — so it is ready to support a motion or argument without additional research.

<Warning>
  Do not upload documents subject to a court-ordered protective order without consulting your attorney first.
</Warning>
