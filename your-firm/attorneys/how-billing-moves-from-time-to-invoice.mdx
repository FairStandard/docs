A client invoice in Vero is never a blank form you fill out from memory. It is built from time that has already been recorded, checked, and locked — the same record you would need if a client ever asked what an hour on their bill actually <span className="kw">covered</span>.

## Time capture starts automatic, and stays correctable

Case-linked work you do inside Vero — drafting, filing, sending a message — accrues time to that case on its own, no timer required. A manual entry covers anything that happens outside what Vero already tracks. Either way, an entry moves through the same states: captured, then submitted, then approved, then locked. Locking is a one-way step. A locked entry is never edited again — a mistake becomes a new entry that corrects it, carrying its own amount and its own record, while the original stays exactly as it was. That matters because a firm's billing history should be able to show, months later, exactly what happened and when, not a version of events that got quietly rewritten.

## An invoice only pulls from time that has been checked

Building an invoice means choosing a client and a case, then pulling in time entries and adding any manual lines. Only an entry that is approved or locked is eligible — a submitted entry that nobody has reviewed yet cannot go on a bill. That rule exists so a busy week of unreviewed time capture never turns into a bill before a person has actually looked at it. Once sent, an invoice can be voided if it went out wrong, but it is never deleted; the record that it existed stays.

## Paying it, and what is not connected yet

An invoice gets marked paid one of two ways: drawn from a client's retainer account, or recorded as having been paid outside Vero. A retainer account holds real money on record — every fund and draw is a permanent entry, and the balance is always the sum of them, never a number typed in separately. Neither payment path actually charges a card; recording that a payment happened and processing one are different jobs, and only the first exists today. The same is true a step further out: connecting your firm's outside accounting software so this billing history flows into your own books automatically is a named capability with nothing built behind it yet. Until it is, the entries you make in Vero still need to be entered into your own accounting system by hand.
