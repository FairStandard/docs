A new engagement starts with the same handful of firm-side steps every time, whether the client came through a referral, the Marketplace, or someone who called you directly. None of it is <span className="kw">complicated</span>, but skipping a step now means going back to fix it later.

## What to do

Add the client to their case first, as the case's client, so they can be assigned to it before anything else happens. If a colleague or staff member is also working the matter, add them to the case the same way — an organization member is not automatically on every case, so this is a separate step even for someone already at your firm. If you plan to bill this client, open a retainer account for the case next; you can leave it unfunded until they actually send money. From there, work the case as you normally would — the time you spend on it starts accruing automatically the moment you touch it inside Vero.

## What Vero does for you

Adding the client and any colleagues to the case takes effect immediately — there is no invitation email to wait on, though it does mean everyone involved needs a Vero account of their own before you can add them. Once the retainer account exists, its balance is tracked as a real ledger from the first entry, so there is never a separate number to reconcile against it later. Billable time capture starts working the moment the case does, without you having to turn anything on.

What Vero does not do yet is send that new client an invitation before they have an account, or give you one screen listing every client across your firm at once — you are working from the case itself, not a combined directory. If your firm uses outside accounting software, this client's billing activity still needs to be entered there separately; that connection is not built.
