Every case you carry has its own <span className="kw">docket</span> and its own timeline, and both work the same way on every matter, so moving between cases doesn't mean relearning how to read either one.

## The docket, case by case

The docket is the court's own register of what happened on a matter: filings, hearings, and service events, each with the court's label and code and results and notes attached. Nothing here is paraphrased in place of the court's wording — a continuance shows up the same way the court's own record would show it, so what you see matches what a clerk would tell you if you called. You can add a note to a docket entry, but the entry itself stays tied to the court's own wording; your note sits alongside it.

## The timeline, per case

The timeline pulls the same docket events together with the case's records into a single chronology. Where the docket is the court's register, the timeline is the whole story of the matter — what happened, in order, whether it came from a filing or from a record you or a client added. A gap in the timeline is information too: months of nothing followed by three events in one week is a pattern worth noticing, and the timeline makes it visible without you having to reconstruct it from a longer list.

## Working across your caseload

Neither the docket nor the timeline changes shape because you carry more than one case. What changes is how you get to them: your waiting-on queue and your dashboard roll information up across every matter, but the docket and timeline you open for any one case are the same detailed view a client on that case would see, not a summarized version built for someone managing several matters at once.
