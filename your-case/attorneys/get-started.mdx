Setting up your <span className="kw">view</span> of a case in Vero takes a few minutes. This guide walks you through adding a case and then shows you where to find what you need across the screens you'll use most: Cases, Case Detail, and Dashboard.

## What you'll learn

- How to add a case to your caseload
- Where to find your dashboard, your docket, and your timeline
- How to run your cross-case waiting-on queue
- How to work through a client's flagged items

## Before you begin

You need a Vero account with an organization membership before you can add a case. If you already have an account and belong to a firm in Vero, sign in and skip ahead.

### Step 1: Add a case to your caseload

From Cases, select **New case**. Enter the caption, the case number, the court, the parties, and the case type. Save it, and the case appears on your Cases list right away. If you belong to more than one organization, the case is tagged to the firm you added it under, and it will not appear on your view of any other firm.

### Step 2: Find your way around Case Detail

Open the case. This one page holds everything about the matter: the docket at the top, the combined timeline below it, and panels for the waiting-on queue and review tracking. Your client sees this exact page for their case — there is no separate, simplified copy for them.

### Step 3: Check your dashboard

Go to Dashboard. It rolls up today's posture across every matter you carry, so you can see where things stand before you open a single case.

### Step 4: Work the docket

Back in Case Detail, open the docket. Every filing, hearing, and service event for that case shows up here, in order, with results and notes, in the court's own wording.

### Step 5: Run your waiting-on queue

Check the waiting-on panel. On its own it's case-scoped, but your cross-case work queue rolls the same information up across every matter you carry, so you can see everything you owe and everything you're owed without opening each case in turn.

## Tips

- Work through a client's flagged items as they arrive. Each one comes bundled with the record, docket entry, or finding it references, so you rarely need to go looking for context.
- Order analysis and witness links only show up on a case once it's been enabled for them — if you don't see one, check the case's setup rather than assuming something is missing.
