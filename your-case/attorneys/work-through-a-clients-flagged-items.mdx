A client can flag anything on their case the moment it catches their attention — a record, a docket entry, a finding. Each flag arrives on your side with the item and its surrounding context already bundled, so you're not starting from a bare note.

## Before you begin

You don't need the client to explain a flag in full before you open it. Their note is a starting point; the record, docket entry, or finding it's attached to is already there for you to review alongside it.

1. Open the case and go to its flagged list.
2. Select a flagged item. The record, docket entry, or finding it references opens alongside the client's note on why it matters.
3. Decide whether it needs your action, a staff member's follow-up, or nothing further, based on what you and the note show.
4. Resolve it — act on it, assign it, or clear it — and record what you decided.

## What happens next

Clearing a flag marks the follow-up as done. It doesn't delete the underlying item or the client's note, so the history of what was flagged and why stays on the case.

If a case is shared with self-represented status changing to represented, or the reverse, flags already on the case carry forward — nothing a client flagged is lost when representation changes.
