Review <span className="kw">tracking</span> shows whether a client has opened and gone through what landed in front of them, so you know before a call whether they've actually seen it rather than assuming.

1. Open the case and go to review tracking.
2. Check each item's status — read or not yet opened — before you rely on the client having seen it.
3. Add an annotation on an item if you want to point the client to a specific part of it, such as a paragraph in an order or a page in a filing.
4. Confirm the client is caught up before a hearing or a call where the item is likely to come up.

## What happens next

Status updates as soon as the client opens the item — there's no delay and no separate step for them to mark it read. If a document has been sitting unopened for a while, that's a fact worth raising with the client directly rather than assuming they've seen it.

If you represent the same client on more than one matter, review status is tracked case by case, not across the client's whole relationship with your firm — a document read on one matter doesn't mark a similar one on another matter as read. Treat each case's review list on its own, especially before a hearing or filing deadline tied to that specific case.

Annotations you add stay attached to the item, not to the client's account, so they carry forward if the case moves between attorneys at your firm — the next person on the matter sees what was flagged for the client's attention without having to ask.
