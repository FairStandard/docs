Case Detail is the one page that holds everything about a single case. It looks like several panels, but every one of them draws from the same underlying facts about your case — nothing on this page is a separate record you have to keep in sync with the others.

## The docket

The docket is the court's own register of what happened: filings, hearings, and service events, each with the court's label and code and, next to it, a plain-language description of what the event means. If a hearing was continued, the docket shows the continuance the same way the court's own record would, so what you see here matches what a clerk would tell you if you called. You can add a note or a comment to any docket entry, but the entry itself always stays tied to the court's own wording — your note sits next to it, not in place of it.

## The timeline

The timeline pulls the same docket events together with your records into a single chronology. Where the docket is the court's register, the timeline is your whole story — what happened, in order, whether it came from a court filing or from something you added yourself. If you want the plain sequence of events without the docket's formatting, the timeline is the view to use. A gap in the timeline is information too: if nothing has happened on your case for months and then three events land in one week, that pattern is visible at a glance instead of buried in a long list.

## What you're waiting on

The waiting-on tracker lists what is owed to you and what you owe — to opposing counsel, the court, or a provider — so nothing sits open without you knowing about it. An empty waiting-on list means nothing is currently outstanding, not that the tracker isn't working.

## What's ready for your review

Incoming filings and orders land on a reading list with your progress tracked, so you always know what you've already gone through and what's still waiting. If you're represented, this is also where documents your attorney sent specifically for your review show up.
