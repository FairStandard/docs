Anything on your case — a record, a docket entry, a finding — can be flagged the moment you notice it matters, instead of waiting until you remember to mention it later.

## Before you begin

You don't need to know exactly why something matters before you flag it. A short note is enough; you or your attorney can work out the details afterward.

1. Open the item you want to flag — a record, a docket entry, or a finding on your case.
2. Select **Flag for review**.
3. Add a short note explaining why it matters. A sentence is enough — "this contradicts the order from March" or "check this date against the filing deadline."
4. Submit the flag.

The item now shows up on your flagged list, with your note attached.

## What happens next

If you're self-represented, the flag stays on your own list until you clear it. If you hire an attorney later, every flag you've made transfers to them automatically — you don't lose the work of having noticed something.

If you're represented, your attorney sees the flag along with the related records and analysis already bundled together, so they don't have to go looking for the context behind your note.

You can clear a flag at any time once it's been dealt with. Clearing it doesn't delete the item itself or your note — it just marks the follow-up as done.
