Setting up a case in Vero takes a few minutes. This guide walks you through creating it and then shows you where to find what you need on the three screens you'll use most: Cases, Case Detail, and Dashboard.

## What you'll learn

- How to create a case
- Where to find your dashboard, your docket, and your timeline
- How to check what you're waiting on
- How to flag something for follow-up

## Before you begin

You need a Vero account before you can create a case. Go to [app.fairstandard.com](https://app.fairstandard.com) and follow the sign-up steps to set one up. If you already have an account, sign in and skip ahead.

### Step 1: Create your case

From Cases, select **New case**. Give it a name for your own reference — this label does not appear on anything filed with the court. Choose the state where the case is filed and the case type, such as custody, divorce, child support, or modification. Save it, and the case appears on your Cases list right away.

### Step 2: Find your way around Case Detail

Open the case you just created. This one page is where everything about the case lives: the docket at the top, the combined timeline below it, and panels for what you're waiting on and what's ready for your review. If you're represented, your attorney sees this exact page — there is no separate, simplified copy.

### Step 3: Check your dashboard

Go to Dashboard. It shows where your case stands right now and what it needs from you today, without you having to open the case itself to find out.

### Step 4: Read your docket

Back in Case Detail, open the docket. Every filing, hearing, and service event for your case shows up here, in the order it happened, in plain language next to the court's own label and code.

### Step 5: Set up your waiting-on list

Check the waiting-on panel. It's empty until something is actually owed — that's expected, not a sign something is missing. As filings and requests come in, this is where you'll see what you're waiting on and from whom.

## Tips

- Flag anything that looks important as you go. A flag takes one tap and a short note, and it stays on the case whether you're working alone or with an attorney.
- If you're self-represented now but hire an attorney later, everything you've flagged transfers to them — you don't lose your own work by adding representation.
