Your case lives on three screens. Cases is the list of every case you have. Case Detail is the one page that holds everything about a single case — the docket, the timeline, what you're waiting on, and what needs your attention. Dashboard is the summary that greets you when you sign in, showing where things stand right now. This page explains what each screen shows and how they connect.

## How it works

A Case in Vero is one continuous record, not a folder you have to keep organized yourself. Every court event, every record, and everything you are waiting on attaches to the same Case, so the docket, the timeline, and your waiting-on list are three views of the same underlying facts rather than three separate lists to maintain.

Vero shows the same Case to everyone connected to it. If you are represented, your attorney sees the same docket, the same timeline, and the same flags you do — there is no separate "client view" with less information. If you are self-represented, the Case Detail page is yours alone to work from, and it works the same way it would for an attorney: nothing about the page is simplified because no one else is looking at it with you.

Each event, record, or order on the page carries the court's own label and code where one exists, alongside a plain-language description of what it means. You always see both — the label a form or a clerk would recognize, and a sentence that says what it actually means for your case.

## What you can do here

- **Track your case.** Set it up once — name, court, and case type — and Vero keeps its status and Phase current from there.
- **See the whole story.** Combined timeline lays out every court event and record in order, so you can see what happened and when.
- **Read the docket.** Every filing, hearing, and service event shows up here, with results and notes, in plain language.
- **Know what you're waiting on.** The waiting-on tracker shows what is owed to you and what you owe — to opposing counsel, the court, or providers — so nothing sits open without you knowing it.
- **Work through what's ready for review.** Incoming filings and orders show up on a reading list, with your progress tracked, so nothing goes unread.
- **Read your court orders in plain language.** Order analysis breaks a court order's provisions down into sentences you can actually use.
- **Flag anything that matters.** One flag, with a note on why, and the item is marked for follow-up — yours if you're on your own, your attorney's if you're represented.
- **Share evidence with a witness.** A controlled link lets a witness see exactly the records you choose, nothing more.
- **Check your dashboard.** One page shows where your case stands and what it needs from you right now.

## Who sees what

Case relationship, not case type, decides what a person sees on a Case. A represented party sees their case, who else is connected to it, and how far along it is. A self-represented party sees and manages the same information directly, with no attorney in the loop. Neither view withholds analysis from the other — a self-represented person sees the same findings and order analysis an attorney would see, and decides for themselves what to do with a flagged item.

## Availability

Order analysis and witness links depend on what your case has been set up with. If a page or button described here is not on your screen, your case has not been enabled for that capability yet — that is a fact about your case's setup, not a mistake, and it does not affect anything else on the page.
