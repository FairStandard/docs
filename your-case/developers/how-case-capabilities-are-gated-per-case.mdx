Case tracking and the docket are open to every case relationship on `case-api` — there is nothing to check before you call them. Three capabilities in this section are different: order analysis and witness links require the Advanced <span className="kw">tier</span> and can be turned on or off per case, and workload estimation requires Premium Professional and is visible only to an owner or attorney org role. `entitlements-api` is what decides all three, and it decides them the same way every time.

## A Grant is an authorization, not an answer

`entitlements-api` stores `Grant` rows — one payer's authorization for a capability, a whole tier, or a whole pro-bono case. A grant existing does not mean access is currently allowed. `resolve()` is the door that turns a set of grants into a yes or no, at the moment of use, for a given workspace, case, and beneficiary. Nothing is cached: canon rules that feature access is resolved at use time, never hard-owned, so `resolve()` re-fetches active grants on every call rather than remembering a prior answer.

## Tiers are cumulative, capabilities are a registry string

`cap.order-analysis`, `cap.witness-links`, and `cap.estimation` are registry strings, seeded into `entitlements-api` from the canon capability register as data — never a hardcoded list in the service's own code. A `Grant` with a `tier` set (rather than a specific `capability_key`) covers every capability at or below that tier: an Advanced-tier grant covers order analysis and witness links without a separate grant for each one, because the tier ladder is cumulative. `resolve()` expands that at read time against the capability's own `tier_required`, so raising or lowering a grant's tier changes what it covers immediately, with no re-issuing of capability-level grants.

## Per-case enablement is a separate axis from tier

Order analysis and witness links are marked `per_case_enablable` in the register: an org can hold the Advanced tier and still have the capability off on a specific case, deliberately. `resolve()`'s `case_id` argument is what makes that check possible — pass it, and a case that has not been turned on for the capability resolves to denied even under an Advanced-tier grant that would otherwise cover it. Omit it, and you get the org-level answer only, which is the wrong question to ask before you show a case-scoped feature.

## What a denial means

A denial from `resolve()` is a fact about the case's or the org's current setup, not an error condition and not a reason to retry. Treat it the same way the litigant and attorney pages already do: the feature is not enabled here yet, and that says nothing about whether anything else on the case is working correctly.
