---
title: "2. The trust ledger is append-only; paying from retainer is one atomic transaction"
description: "Date: 2026-08-30"
---

Date: 2026-08-30

## Status

Accepted

## Context

A retainer account holds client trust funds. Every jurisdiction's trust
accounting rules rest on the same property: you can always reconstruct
exactly what happened to a client's money, in order, without anything
having been silently changed after the fact. A ledger with UPDATE or
DELETE paths cannot make that promise — a bug or a bad actor could
rewrite history and there would be no way to tell.

Separately, paying an invoice out of a retainer is a single legal event
(draw the trust funds, mark the invoice paid) even though it is two
database writes. If those writes could commit separately, a crash
between them would either lose the client's funds (drawn but invoice
still shows unpaid) or misstate money the firm doesn't have (invoice
paid, but no trust funds actually moved).

## Decision

`LedgerEntry` rows are never updated or deleted anywhere in this
service — `store.py` has no method that does either. A correction is
always a new row: `reverse_ledger_entry` writes a reversing entry that
carries `reversal_of_id` pointing at the entry it undoes; the original
stays exactly as written. The account balance is never a stored column
— it is `SUM(signed_cents)` over the account's entries, computed on
every read, so the balance can never drift from the entries that are
its only truth.

`Store.pay_invoice_from_retainer` writes the draw `LedgerEntry` and
flips the `Invoice.status` to `paid` inside one `AsyncSession` /
transaction, committed once. Either both happen or neither does.

## Consequences

Every retainer balance is fully auditable from the entry history alone.
The cost is that "editing a mistake" always means writing one more row,
and callers reading history need to understand a reversal pair rather
than seeing a value simply change.
