Date: 2026-08-30

## Status

Accepted. Amended by ADR 0005: this ADR's "platform charges" exclusion
was too wide — this service *is* the home of platform subscription
billing (money owed). What still stands, unchanged: entitlement
*state* (what an org's tier or add-ons entitle it to access) has no
model, table, or door here.

## Context

FairStandard already tracks org-level entitlements — what an
organization pays the platform for (tiers, add-ons, per-case flags).
Client invoicing is a different lifecycle entirely: it is money the
firm's *client* owes the firm for legal work, denominated in the firm's
own currency and terms, moving through draft/sent/paid, and touching a
trust ledger that is legally distinct from the firm's operating funds.
Folding the two into one billing model would mean every entitlement
change risks a client-money bug, and every client-billing change risks
an org-payment bug.

Canon is explicit: the org always pays the platform; clients pay legal
invoices. Two payers, two lifecycles.

## Decision

`billing-payments-api` owns exactly one of these lifecycles: client
invoicing, retainer trust accounting, and the billable time that feeds
invoices. It has no model, table, or door for org-level entitlements,
tiers, or platform charges — that stays wherever entitlements already
live. The two services never share a database, and nothing in this
service assumes it can read or write entitlement state.

## Consequences

A bug in retainer draws can never corrupt what an org owes the
platform, and vice versa. The cost is that anything genuinely shared
between the two lifecycles (e.g. "is client billing enabled for this
workspace") has to be asked for, not assumed — this service treats that
as external state it is told, not state it owns.
