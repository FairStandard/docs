Ruled 2026-08-30, following the platform's slicing rulings (six services,
own Postgres each, private queues): identity — accounts, organizations,
membership, case teams, and the audience registries — is carved out as its
own service with its own Postgres database, not folded into any other
carved service.

Two design laws travel with the carve-out and are locked, not open for
re-litigation by a future caller:

1. **The organization is always the paying customer.** Never the account.
   Billing amounts live elsewhere later, but the payer role is structural
   here: `Organization` carries jurisdiction and pro-bono status because
   those are the org's own facts, not derived from any one member.

2. **Every vocabulary this service exposes is an extensible registry
   stored as data**, not a closed enum or a `CHECK` constraint. Org role
   (owner/attorney/staff/provider) and case relationship
   (representing_professional/represented_party/self_represented_party/
   external_participant) ship as seeded rows in registry tables. Adding a
   value is an insert. There is no `user_type` column anywhere in this
   service — org role lives on the membership, case relationship lives on
   the case-team record, and presentation register is derived at read
   time, never stored.

Other services that need to know who someone is call this service's
`/internal/verify` endpoint rather than keeping their own copy of account
or membership state.
