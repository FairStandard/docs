---
title: "1. Channels are adapters; fan-out never knows which one it holds"
description: "Date: 2026-08-30"
---

Date: 2026-08-30

## Status

Accepted

## Context

The platform needs one delivery layer, not one per channel, and the set
of channels will grow (in-app now, email and push behind a seam this
land, others later — SMS, Slack, whatever a future integration needs).
If fan-out branched on channel identity, every new channel would mean
editing the one piece of logic every notification flows through.

## Decision

Every channel — including in-app — implements the same `Channel`
protocol: a registry-string `name` and an async `deliver(event)`.
Fan-out holds a list of channels, checks the recipient's preference for
each, and calls `deliver` on the ones allowed. It never inspects which
channel it is calling.

`in_app` is real: `deliver` writes the `Notification` row, so the row
IS the in-app delivery record, not a mirror of one that lives
elsewhere. `email` and `push` are Fakes this land — they record what
they would have sent, with no SMTP client or provider SDK anywhere in
the codebase. A later PR replacing `FakeEmailChannel` with a real
adapter touches only `delivery.py (renamed from channels.py, ADR 0004)` and the `build_fanout` wiring in
`fanout.py`.

## Consequences

Adding a channel is additive: one adapter, one line in the registration
list. Tests exercise real fan-out logic against fakes with no test
double baked into fan-out itself. The cost is that email/push send
nothing real yet — acceptable, since the task for this land is the
seam, not a specific provider integration.
