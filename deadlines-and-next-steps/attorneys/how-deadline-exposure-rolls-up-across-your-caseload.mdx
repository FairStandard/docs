A deadline's urgency is computed once, from the same facts regardless of which case it's on: how soon it's due, and what's already been done toward it. What differs by view is not the computation but the <span className="kw">aggregate</span> — a client sees urgency for their case alone, while you see it summed across everything you carry.

## Where urgency comes from

Every deadline ties back to something that created it — a rule that applies to the case, a court order, a filed motion, or the court's own hearing schedule. Urgency isn't a separate judgment call layered on top; it's computed from the deadline's date and from what's already been completed toward it, the same way on every case regardless of practice area or court.

That consistency is what makes exposure comparable across a caseload at all. A response due in four days on a family law matter and a filing due in four days on a civil matter carry the same urgency status for the same reason — the computation doesn't know or care which practice area the case belongs to, only how soon the date is and how much of the work toward it is already done.

## How exposure aggregates across a caseload

Your Calendar and your Next Steps queue both read from every case you're assigned to, across every organization you belong to. A hearing at one firm and a filing deadline at another land on the same list, each carrying a Tag naming the organization it belongs to. If you're an owner, exposure aggregates one level higher still — summed across every attorney at the firm, not just your own assignments.

## Why a client's view stays separate

A client's Deadline view stops at their own case — they see the same computed urgency you do for that one matter, but nothing about your other cases, and nothing about exposure at the firm level. That separation isn't a simplified copy of your view; it's the same underlying facts, scoped to what's actually theirs. Neither of you is working from a stale or filtered version of the other's information — the difference is scope, not accuracy.
