Deadlines and next steps lives on two screens here too, but what you see is scoped to your whole caseload rather than one client's case. Calendar rolls up every hearing, filing deadline, and response window across every matter you carry. Next Steps is your own queue — a professional list, separate from what a client sees on their side of the same case. This page explains what each screen shows and how a deadline turns into <span className="kw">exposure</span> you can track before it turns into a missed date.

## How it works

A Deadline in Vero always ties back to something that created it — a rule that applies to the case, a court order, a filed motion, or the court's own hearing schedule. That link is what lets the docket entry, the plain-language description, and the deadline itself stay in sync: when a continuance or an amended order moves a date, the deadline updates once and everything tied to it moves with it, rather than leaving you to reconcile several copies by hand.

Each deadline also carries a computed urgency, not just a due date. A response due in four days ranks differently than a hearing six weeks out that's already prepared for, and that ranking is what drives your Next Steps queue rather than a plain chronological list.

Calendar shows this across every case you're on, in every organization you belong to. A hearing at one firm and a filing deadline at another sit on the same calendar, each carrying a Tag naming the organization it belongs to, so you always know which matter a date is attached to before you act on it.

## What you can do here

- **Track deadline exposure.** See every dated obligation across your caseload, each with a computed urgency, not just a due date.
- **Work your caseload calendar.** Every hearing, filing deadline, and response window across every case you carry, in one place.
- **Run your own Next Steps queue.** A professional-side list of deadlines, filings, and client follow-ups — distinct from what any client sees.
- **Set court prep tasks.** For a represented client, build the list of tasks tied to their hearing; a self-represented client's tasks tie to Learning Center lessons instead.
- **Draft a filing.** Structured blocks and auto-numbered exhibits, ready for a client to review before anything goes to the court.

## Who sees what

Deadline exposure rolls up the same way for everyone on a case, but only you see it summed across your whole caseload — a client's Deadline view stops at their own case, while yours spans every matter you carry, across every firm you're a member of.

A reminder you set on a deadline is yours alone. Your client sets their own reminders on the same deadline independently, on their own schedule, and neither of you sees the other's reminder settings — setting one for yourself doesn't put anything on a client's calendar, and their reminder doesn't put anything on yours.

What a client sees in Next Steps is not what you see in yours. Their list folds in whatever their attorney is coordinating, alongside their own deadlines, court prep, and filing; yours is a separate professional queue built from your caseload, not a mirror of any one client's checklist.
