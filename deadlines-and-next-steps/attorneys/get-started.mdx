Finding your way around deadlines and next steps takes a few minutes once you know it's scoped to your whole <span className="kw">caseload</span>, not one case at a time. This guide walks you through checking deadline exposure, working your queue, and setting a court prep task for a client.

## What you'll learn

- How to see deadline exposure across every case you carry
- How your Next Steps queue differs from a client's checklist
- How to set a court prep task for a represented client
- How to open the filing workspace and start a draft
- Where firm-wide deadline exposure fits, if you're an owner

## Before you begin

You need at least one case assigned to you before anything appears here. If you haven't been added to a case yet, ask whoever manages your firm's client roster to add you first. Once you're on a case, Vero starts populating deadlines from the events already on its docket.

### Step 1: Open your caseload calendar

From the sidebar, select **Calendar**. You'll see every hearing, filing deadline, response window, and other dated obligation across every case you carry, laid out in order and tagged with the case and firm it belongs to.

### Step 2: Check deadline exposure

Select any deadline to open its detail view. Each one shows the date it's due, a plain-language description, the rule or order that created it — quoted the way the court wrote it — and a computed urgency status rather than just a date on a page.

### Step 3: Open your Next Steps queue

Go to **Next Steps**. This is your own professional queue: deadlines, filings, and client follow-ups ranked by urgency, with a line explaining why each item sits where it does. It's a separate list from anything a client sees on their side of the same case.

### Step 4: Set a court prep task for a client

Open an upcoming hearing on a represented client's case and find its prep task list. Add a task the client should complete before the hearing — reviewing a document, gathering an exhibit, reading a specific lesson. For a self-represented client, prep tasks tie to Learning Center lessons instead, so there's nothing for you to assign there.

### Step 5: Open the filing workspace

From Case Detail, open the filing workspace for a case. Start a draft in structured blocks, with exhibits numbered automatically as you add them. Share the draft with a represented client for their review before anything is filed.

## Tips

- Check deadline exposure before you check your queue — the queue is ranked by the same urgency you'll see there, so the order will make more sense.
- A court prep task you set only reaches a represented client. If a client is self-represented, point them to the relevant lesson instead of trying to assign a task.
