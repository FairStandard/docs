Deadlines and next steps lives on two screens. Calendar lays out every court date on your case in one place. Next Steps turns those dates into a plain list of what to do and when. This page explains what each screen shows and how a <span className="kw">deadline</span> becomes something you can act on.

## How it works

Every deadline on your case comes from a rule the court applies to your case — a response window, a filing deadline, a statute of limitations, a hearing date. Vero tracks a deadline the same way no matter where it came from: a court order, a filed motion, or the court's own calendar. Each one carries a date, a plain description of what is due, and the rule or order that set it, shown alongside the court's own wording rather than in place of it.

Deadlines appear automatically as events are logged on your case. You do not add a filing deadline by hand after a motion is filed against you — it shows up on its own, tied to the filing that created it.

Next Steps reads that same set of deadlines and turns it into a short, ordered list of actions. A response due in four days with nothing started ranks above a hearing six weeks out that is already prepared for — and the list says why, rather than leaving you to guess which item actually matters most right now.

If a court date moves — a continuance, an amended order, a new filing — updating it once moves everything that depended on it. You are not left tracking a chain of stale dates by hand after one thing changes.

## What you can do here

- **See your court dates.** Calendar shows every hearing, filing deadline, and response window on your case in one place, not scattered across separate notices.
- **Track every deadline.** Each one shows its date, what is due, and the rule or order that created it.
- **Work from a plain list of next steps.** Next Steps tells you what to do and why it matters, ordered by how soon it is due.
- **Prepare for a hearing.** Court prep breaks the run-up to a hearing into a short list of tasks, tied to what you need to know before you go.
- **Work on a filing.** If you are represented, review a draft filing your attorney shares with you. If you are self-represented, build your own filing step by step, with guided, informational help along the way.
- **Set a reminder.** Attach a reminder to any deadline so it reaches you by email, as an in-app notification, or both, at the intervals you choose.

## Who sees what

A represented party's deadlines and next steps sit alongside whatever their attorney is coordinating — the same dates, folded into one checklist rather than kept in two places. Court prep tasks for a represented party are set by the attorney; for a self-represented party, they are tied instead to relevant Learning Center lessons, so preparation does not depend on someone else assigning it. Filing works the same way: a represented party reviews what their attorney drafts, while a self-represented party builds a filing on their own, one step at a time.

## Availability

Court prep and filing depend on what your case has been set up with. If a page or button described here is not on your screen, your case has not been enabled for that capability yet — that is a fact about your case's setup, not a mistake, and it does not affect anything else on the page.
