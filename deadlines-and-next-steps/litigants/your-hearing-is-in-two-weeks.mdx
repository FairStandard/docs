Aug 12, 2026 · 14 days out — that is what a hearing two weeks away looks like on your calendar. There is time to prepare, but not so much time that the <span className="kw">countdown</span> stops mattering.

## What to do

Open the hearing on Calendar and read its full detail — the courtroom or location if one is set, and any deadlines tied to it, like a document that has to be filed before the hearing rather than at it. Check Next Steps for anything ranked at the top of your list; a filing deadline sitting a few days ahead of the hearing itself usually outranks the hearing for now, and the list will say so. If your case has court preparation turned on, open the hearing's prep task list and work through it rather than leaving it until the last few days.

## What Vero does for you

Vero keeps the hearing, its prep tasks, and any deadline that falls before it connected to each other, so working through one does not mean losing track of the others. As the date gets closer, Next Steps re-ranks your list so the hearing and anything tied to it move up automatically — you do not have to keep checking the calendar to notice that the window is narrowing. What Vero does not do is tell you what to say or argue at the hearing itself; prep tasks explain the process and what to expect, not what your position should be.

<Note>
  Vero explains legal process. It does not give legal advice.
</Note>
