What you do here depends on whether you are represented or self-represented, but both start in the same place. A <span className="kw">filing</span> workspace on Case Detail holds whatever needs to go to the court next, whether someone else drafted it or you are building it yourself.

1. From Case Detail, open the filing workspace.
2. If you are represented, look for a draft your attorney has shared with you. Open it to read through what has been drafted so far.
3. If you are self-represented, select **Start a filing** and choose what you are filing. Vero walks you through it in structured blocks, with guided, informational help at each one.
4. Add anything the filing needs — an exhibit, a supporting record — from your existing records rather than uploading it twice.
5. Review the filing in full before it goes anywhere. If you are represented, your review does not replace your attorney's; if you are self-represented, this is your only review before it is submitted, so read it closely.

## Before you begin

Filing only appears if your case has been set up with that capability. If Case Detail does not show a filing workspace, your case has not been enabled for it yet — that is a fact about your case's setup, not an error.

## What happens next

A filing you have reviewed or built stays in the workspace, linked to any deadline it responds to, so you can find it again from either the filing workspace or the deadline itself. Vero helps you assemble a filing correctly. It does not tell you what to argue or whether a filing is the right move for your case — that judgment stays with you and, if you are represented, with your attorney.

<Note>
  Vero explains legal process. It does not give legal advice.
</Note>
