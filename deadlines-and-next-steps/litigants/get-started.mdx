Finding your way around your <span className="kw">calendar</span> and your Next Steps list takes a few minutes. This guide walks you through checking a deadline, setting a reminder, and reading what Next Steps recommends and why.

## What you'll learn

- How to find a deadline on your calendar
- What each deadline shows and where it comes from
- How to set a reminder for a deadline
- How to read what Next Steps recommends
- Where court prep and filing show up if your case has them

## Before you begin

You need a case set up in Vero before anything can appear here. If you have not created your case yet, see [Get started with your case](/your-case/litigants/get-started) first. Once your case exists, Vero starts populating deadlines from the events already on your docket.

### Step 1: Open your calendar

From Case Detail, select **Calendar**. You will see every hearing, filing deadline, response window, and other dated obligation on your case, laid out in order. If your case is new, this list may be short — it grows as filings, orders, and hearings are logged.

### Step 2: Open a deadline

Select any deadline to see its detail view. Each one shows the date it is due, a plain-language description of what is due, and the rule or court order that created it, quoted the way the court wrote it. If the deadline came from a specific filing or hearing, that source is linked here too.

### Step 3: Set a reminder

From the deadline's detail view, select **Set reminder**. Choose email, an in-app notification, or both, and pick when you want to hear about it — for example, 14 days before and again 3 days before. You can set more than one interval on the same deadline.

### Step 4: Check Next Steps

Go to Next Steps. This list turns your deadlines into a short set of actions, ordered by how soon each one is due, with a line explaining why it is ranked where it is. Select any item to see the deadline or event behind it.

### Step 5: Look for court prep and filing

If your case has been set up with court preparation, a hearing on your calendar carries its own short list of prep tasks — open the hearing to see them. If your case has filing turned on, Case Detail carries a filing workspace where you can review a draft your attorney shared or build your own filing, depending on whether you are represented or self-represented.

<Note>
  Vero explains legal process. It does not give legal advice.
</Note>

## Tips

- Set a reminder as soon as a deadline appears rather than waiting until it feels urgent. A reminder set with two weeks of lead time is more useful than one set with two days left.
- Read the rule or order behind a deadline, not just the date. The plain-language description tells you what is due; the quoted rule tells you exactly what the court is enforcing.
