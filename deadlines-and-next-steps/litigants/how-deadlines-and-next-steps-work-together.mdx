A deadline is a date. Next Steps <span className="kw">translates</span> that date into something you can actually act on — a specific task, ranked against everything else you have open right now. The two are built to stay connected rather than to be read on their own.

## Where a deadline comes from

Every deadline on your case is tied to something that created it: a rule that applies to your case, a court order, a filed motion, or the court's own hearing schedule. The connection is not just internal bookkeeping — it is what lets Vero show you the actual rule or order alongside the plain-language description, so you can see both what is due and why it is due on that date.

## How Next Steps ranks a deadline

Next Steps does not just list deadlines in date order. It orders them by urgency and by what is already done: a response due in four days with nothing started outranks a hearing six weeks out that already has its prep tasks finished. Each item on the list carries a short reason, so you are never looking at an unranked task with no explanation for why it sits where it does.

## When a deadline changes

A continuance, an amended order, or a new filing can move a deadline's date after it has already appeared. When that happens, the deadline updates once, and anything downstream that depended on it — a reminder, a Next Steps item, a court prep task tied to a hearing — moves with it. You are not left reconciling several separate copies of the same date by hand, and you do not have to remember every place a date was referenced before you can trust that it is current.

## Why the two stay separate screens

Calendar and Next Steps show the same underlying facts, but they answer different questions. Calendar answers "what is coming and when" — every date in order, with nothing filtered out. Next Steps answers "what should I actually do right now" — a shorter, ranked list that leaves out anything already handled or not yet actionable. Reading only Calendar can leave you with a long list and no sense of priority; reading only Next Steps can leave you without the full picture of what is still ahead. Together, one shows you everything and the other tells you where to start.
