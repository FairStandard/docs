Your case can build a simple <span className="kw">picture</span> of how communication is going — not a score or a grade, just a plain account of what was asked, what was answered, and what wasn't.

## What the picture shows

The picture draws from the same messages and deadlines already on your case: how long it took to get a response after something was asked, and whether a requirement a court order set — like turning over a document by a certain date — was met. It updates as new messages and events land on your case; there's nothing separate for you to fill out, and no form to submit before it reflects what actually happened.

Response times are shown as a trend, not a single number. One slow reply during a week when a hearing was also on the calendar reads differently than the same delay happening every week for a month, and the picture is built to show that difference rather than flatten it into an average.

## What counts as a violation

A violation, here, means a requirement set by a court order wasn't met by the date the order set — a document that wasn't provided on time, or a step the order required that didn't happen. Vero flags it as a violation because the order said so, not because Vero is judging the other side's conduct. Vero also doesn't decide how serious a violation is or what should happen next; it shows the requirement, the date, and whether it was met, the same way it shows any other deadline on your case.

What to do about a violation is a decision for you and your attorney. This page, and the picture it feeds, only describe what happened against what the order required — they don't recommend a response.

## Turning it on for your case

Cooperation tracking is an add-on for your case, not something every case has by default. If you don't see a cooperation picture on your Messages screen, your case hasn't been enabled for it yet — ask your attorney, or check your case settings if you're self-represented. Turning it on doesn't change anything about your existing messages or deadlines; it starts reading the ones already on your case from that point forward.

</Note>
