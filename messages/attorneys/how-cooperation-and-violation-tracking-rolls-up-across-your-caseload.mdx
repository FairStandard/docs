Cooperation tracking builds the same picture on every case it's turned on for — response times and whether a court order's requirements were met — but for you it isn't a single case's story. It's one <span className="kw">signal</span> among several across a caseload, and for a firm owner it aggregates further still.

## What builds the picture on one case

The picture draws from the messages and deadlines already on a case: how long it took to get a response after something was asked, and whether a requirement a court order set — a document turned over by a certain date, a step the order required — was met by that date. It updates as new messages and events land on the case; there's nothing separate to fill out, and the picture reflects what actually happened rather than what was reported after the fact.

Response times show as a trend, not a single number. One slow reply during a week with a hearing on the calendar reads differently than the same delay repeating every week for a month, and the trend is what surfaces that difference on a case you're watching for it.

## What counts as a violation

A violation means a requirement set by a court order wasn't met by the date the order set. Vero flags it because the order said so, not because Vero is judging conduct — it doesn't decide how serious a violation is or what should happen next. It shows the requirement, quoted the way the court wrote it, the date, and whether it was met, the same way it shows any other deadline on the case.

## How it aggregates across your caseload and your firm

Your own view sums cooperation signals across every case you're assigned to, so a pattern on one matter doesn't have to be spotted case by case — a client whose responses have slowed on two separate matters shows up as two flagged trends rather than two unrelated facts you'd otherwise have to notice independently. If you're a firm owner, the same signals roll up one level higher: across every case the firm carries, not just your own assignments, so a pattern involving several attorneys' matters is visible from one view.

Turning tracking on for a case doesn't change anything about the case's existing messages or deadlines; it starts reading the ones already there from that point forward, and it's an add-on set per case rather than something every case has by default.
