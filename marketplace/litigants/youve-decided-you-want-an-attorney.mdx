Handling a case on your own is a real option, and so is bringing someone else in partway through. If you've reached the point where you want an attorney, or another kind of legal service provider, the Marketplace is where you start <span className="kw">looking</span>.

## What to do

Open the Marketplace and filter the directory by specialty, jurisdiction, and service type to see who covers your situation. Read a few listings before reaching out — the headline, biography, and published specialties tell you what a provider says they focus on. When you find someone worth talking to, send a consultation request with a short, specific message: what your case involves, what you're looking for (a one-time consultation, ongoing representation, or something narrower), and how they can reach you back.

You can send requests to more than one listing if you want to compare responses — nothing limits you to one at a time, and each request is private to the provider you sent it to.

## What Vero does for you

Vero shows you only listings that are currently active, filtered to the specialty, jurisdiction, and service type you asked for, and keeps your request private to the one provider you sent it to. It also keeps a plain record of whether your request is still queued, was accepted, or was declined, so you're not left guessing.

Deciding whether to bring someone in, and who to choose, is a decision only you can make — Vero shows you who's listed and tracks your requests, but doesn't weigh in on whether hiring an attorney is right for your case or which one to pick.

<Note>
  Vero explains legal process. It does not give legal advice.
</Note>
