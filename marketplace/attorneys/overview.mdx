Your Marketplace listing is what puts you in front of someone who is looking for an attorney or provider and does not yet know your name. This page covers what a listing shows, what it takes to make it <span className="kw">visible</span>, and what happens on your end when someone reaches out.

## How it works

A listing is your presence in the Marketplace: a display name, a headline, a biography, and the specialties, jurisdictions, and service kinds you choose to publish. You set it up under your organization's own workspace, whether you practice solo or as part of a firm.

A new listing starts as a draft. Nothing on it is visible in the public directory yet. Making it visible takes one more step: activating it. Activation checks whether your organization has an active Marketplace subscription on file. If it does, the listing goes live. If it does not, activation is refused and the listing stays a draft — that is a billing question for whoever manages your organization's account, not something wrong with what you wrote on the listing itself.

Once your listing is active, anyone browsing the directory who filters by a specialty, jurisdiction, or service kind you have published can find it. Filtering matches an exact category, not the words inside your biography, so what you check off is what actually surfaces you.

When someone finds your listing and wants to talk, they send a consultation request with a short message and a way to reach them back. That request lands in a queue tied to your listing — no other attorney or provider in the directory ever sees it, and nothing about receiving one changes your case list or your calendar. Deciding to accept or decline is entirely yours.

## What you can do here

- Build and edit your listing's profile: display name, headline, biography, specialties, jurisdictions, and service kinds.
- Activate your listing once your organization's subscription is active, and deactivate it any time you want to stop appearing in the directory.
- See every consultation request sent to your listing, in one queue.
- Accept or decline a consultation request.

## Who sees what

Your public profile — what someone browsing the directory sees — never shows your billing status or how your organization has your access set up. A consultation request sent to your listing is scoped to that listing, not to you personally: anyone at your organization with access to Marketplace tools for it can see the same queue you see, not just the person the request was meant for. No other provider in the directory, and nobody outside your organization, ever sees a request that was not sent to them.

## Availability

Today's specialties and service kinds cover family law: divorce, child custody, child support, adoption, domestic violence, and mediation, alongside service kinds like a consultation, full representation, document review, limited-scope representation, and mediation services. More get added over time, as an addition, never a change to what is already published.

Accepting a request is real, and Vero keeps a permanent record that it happened. What Vero does not yet do is connect that person to a case or open a message thread between you automatically — that part is not built yet. Plan on arranging next steps with them directly, the same way you would after any other consultation you found on your own.
