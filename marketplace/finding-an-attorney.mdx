If you are handling your case on your own and decide you want professional legal representation — or if you simply want to consult with an attorney before deciding — the Vero Marketplace gives you a direct path to attorneys who practice in your jurisdiction and case type. Everything you have already built in your case carries over the moment they connect.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Marketplace">
    From your dashboard or any case, click **Marketplace** in the navigation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Filter by Jurisdiction and Case Type">
    Select your state and case type (e.g., Custody, Divorce) to see attorneys whose practice matches your situation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Browse Profiles">
    Each attorney profile shows their practice areas, jurisdiction(s), languages spoken, and how to contact them for a consultation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Request a Consultation">
    Click **Request Consultation** on any profile. You can include a brief description of your situation. The attorney will receive your request and respond through the platform.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect to Your Case">
    Once you engage an attorney, they can be connected directly to your existing case. They will have immediate access to the evidence vault, assembled law, deadlines, and messaging — no onboarding handoff needed.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What attorneys can see

When connected to a case, an attorney sees the same workspace you see: the evidence vault with all uploaded documents and auto-surfaced patterns, the assembled law with cited statutes and precedents, the full case timeline, upcoming deadlines, next steps, and the shared messaging thread. There is no separate attorney-facing view — your attorney works in the same organized, cited, ready-to-file environment you built together.

<Note>
  If you started your case as self-represented, all of your existing evidence and assembled law carries over seamlessly when you connect an attorney.
</Note>

<Tip>
  When requesting a consultation, describe your case type and the stage you are at (e.g., pre-filing, post-temporary-orders). This helps attorneys quickly assess whether they can help.
</Tip>
