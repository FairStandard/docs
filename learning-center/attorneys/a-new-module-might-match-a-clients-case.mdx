Sep 5, 2026 · a module you didn't go looking for shows up under a client's case as suggested. It's not a general recommendation — it's a <span className="kw">match</span> between something already found on that case and a module written to address it.

## What to do

Open the case and check **Suggested modules** to see what's listed and why — each suggestion names the finding it matched. Read the module yourself first if you want to confirm it's a fit before your client sees it; opening it from the suggestion list doesn't assign it or notify anyone. If it fits, assign it to the client the same way you'd assign anything else from the catalog, tied to that case.

## What Vero does for you

Vero doesn't guess at what a client might find useful. A module is suggested on a case only because something specific already found on that case matches a topic the module's author listed it as addressing — the same list published on the module, checked against that case's own record. If nothing on the case matches an existing module, the suggestions list is empty; that reflects the case's current record, not a gap in the catalog. Nothing about a suggestion changes the case itself, and nothing gets assigned until you make that decision.

  Vero explains legal process. It does not give legal advice.
</Note>
