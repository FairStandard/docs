An assignment is not a live link to a module — it's a copy of one specific version, frozen at the moment you made it. That <span className="kw">freeze</span> is what keeps a client's lesson list stable while you and the module's publisher both keep working.

## Assigning locks in a version

When you assign a module to a client, the assignment records the exact version you were looking at. Publishing a newer version of that module afterward doesn't move the assignment forward to it — the lesson list your client is reading stays exactly as it was the day you assigned it. If you want a client to see the newer version, you assign it again as a separate assignment; the old one still exists, still pinned to the version it started with.

This holds regardless of whether the assignment is tied to a case. An assignment made before a case exists, or one made without a case at all, pins its version the same way one tied to a case does.

## Progress tracks per lesson, not per assignment as a whole

Each lesson inside an assigned module has its own completion record, tied to that specific assignment. Marking one lesson complete doesn't complete the whole thing, and marking the same lesson complete a second time doesn't create a second record — it stays marked once. Because progress is recorded against the assignment, not the module, two different clients working through the same module version each have their own, separate progress.

## There is no single view of your whole caseload's progress yet

Checking who on your caseload has an outstanding assignment today means pulling the workspace's full assignment list, or asking for one client's assignments specifically — there isn't a filter yet for "assignments I made" or "assignments tied to this case." Every assignment your workspace has made shows up in the unfiltered list side by side, whichever attorney made it, so you tell them apart by reading the client and case on each row rather than by a scope the list applies for you.
