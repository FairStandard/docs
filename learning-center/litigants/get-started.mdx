Getting comfortable with the Learning Center takes a few minutes. This guide walks you through browsing the catalog, checking for anything <span className="kw">assigned</span> to you, and marking your first lesson complete.

## What you'll learn

- How to browse the module catalog
- How to open a module assigned to you and read it in order
- How to mark a lesson complete
- How to check whether your case has any suggested modules
- What to expect the first time you visit — an empty list is common, not a sign of a problem

## Before you begin

You need a case set up in Vero. Suggested modules and progress tracking are tied to your case, though the catalog itself is open to browse without one.

### Step 1: Open the Learning Center

From your case, select **Learning Center**. If nothing has been assigned to you yet, you'll see an empty assignments list — that's expected the first time you visit, not a sign anything is missing.

### Step 2: Browse the catalog

Select **Browse modules** to see every current module. Narrow the list by practice area, and by your state if the module is written for a specific jurisdiction.

### Step 3: Open a module

Select a module to open it. Its lessons appear in order — read them one at a time, top to bottom, the same order the author wrote them in.

### Step 4: Check for anything assigned to you

Select **Assigned to you** to see any modules tied to your case specifically. An assigned module stays exactly as it was the day it was assigned, even if a newer version of that module is published later.

### Step 5: Mark a lesson complete

At the end of a lesson, select **Mark complete**. Marking the same lesson complete more than once doesn't cause an error — it just stays marked.

### Step 6: Check your suggested modules

If your case has flagged something a module addresses, it appears under **Suggested for your case**. If you don't see anything there, your case hasn't matched an existing module yet — that can change as your case moves forward.

## Tips

- If a module cites a specific law, the citation you're reading is the current text, not something pasted in when the module was written.
- Progress tracking only applies to modules assigned to you. Reading a module from the open catalog doesn't create a progress record, since there's no assignment behind it.

<Note>
  Vero explains legal process. It does not give legal advice.
</Note>
