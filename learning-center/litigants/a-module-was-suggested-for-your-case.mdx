Sep 5, 2026 · new this week — a module you didn't go looking for shows up under **Suggested for your case**. That's not a random recommendation; it's a <span className="kw">match</span> between something already on your case record and a module written to address it.

## What to do

Open the module the same way you'd open any other — select it and read the lessons in order. It isn't assigned to you, so there's no progress to mark unless someone assigns it to your case afterward; you're free to read it and move on. If more than one module is suggested, there's no required order between them — each one addresses something different on your case, so read whichever matches what you're trying to understand right now.

## What Vero does for you

Vero doesn't guess at what you might find interesting. A module is suggested only because something specific in your case record matches a topic that module is written to cover — the same list a module publishes for itself, checked against what's actually in your case. If nothing on your case matches an existing module, the suggestions list stays empty; that's an honest reflection of your case's record, not a gap in the Learning Center.

<Note>
  Vero explains legal process. It does not give legal advice.
</Note>
