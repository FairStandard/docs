The Learning Center is where you read about how your kind of case actually moves through the process — not a chat window, not advice, just <span className="kw">modules</span> written to explain what's ahead. This page explains what you'll find there and how a module gets in front of you.

## How it works

A module is one piece of learning content — a short guide to one part of the process, broken into a few lessons you read in order. Modules belong to a practice area, and many are written for one state, so a Texas custody module and a California custody module can say different things about the same phase of a case, because the law and the local rules differ. When a module cites a specific law, the citation shown is the current text, kept up to date automatically — you never read an outdated quote.

Modules reach you two ways. You can browse the full catalog yourself, filtered by your practice area and jurisdiction if you want to narrow it down. Or a module can be assigned to you, tied to your case, so it shows up as something to work through rather than something you had to go find.

Once a module is assigned to you, it stays exactly as it was the day it was assigned — later updates to that module land as a new version for new assignments, not underneath your existing progress. If you've started lessons in a module, they won't be renumbered or replaced out from under you.

Some modules connect directly to what's happening in your case. If your case has flagged something specific, a module addressing that same topic can be suggested to you, based on what the case record actually shows — not a guess about what you might want to read.

## What you can do here

- **Browse the catalog.** See every current module, filtered by practice area and, where it applies, your state.
- **Work through an assigned module.** Read each lesson in order and mark it complete as you go.
- **See your progress.** Check which lessons in an assigned module you've already finished.
- **See suggested modules.** If your case has flagged something a module addresses, it can show up here as relevant to read.

## Who sees what

Every workspace reads the same published modules — there's no separate content library per firm or per state that only some people can see. What's assigned to you and what's suggested to you is specific to your case; the catalog itself is shared, open reading for anyone with a case.

## Availability

Suggested modules only appear once your case has findings for something a module addresses. An empty suggestions list isn't a problem with your case — it means nothing yet on your case matches an existing module's topic.
