A module is written content, not a live document — once it exists, it doesn't change under you. Understanding that <span className="kw">stability</span> explains why the Learning Center behaves the way it does.

## A module is a fixed version

When a module is published, that exact version is never edited or deleted afterward. If the content needs to change — a phase gets renamed, a step gets added — that's a new, separate version, not an edit to the old one. The module you're reading today reads the same way tomorrow, unless you open a newer version yourself.

## An assignment locks in one version

When a module is assigned to your case, it pins the exact version that existed at that moment. If a newer version of the same module gets published afterward, your assignment doesn't move to it — the lesson list you're partway through stays exactly as it was, so nothing you've already completed gets renumbered, replaced, or lost. Someone who wants you to see the newer content assigns it to you separately.

## Progress is tracked per lesson, not per module

Each lesson inside an assigned module has its own completion record. Marking one lesson complete doesn't complete the whole module, and marking the same lesson complete a second time doesn't create a duplicate — it just stays marked. This is why closing a module partway through and coming back later shows you exactly where you left off.

## A citation resolves live, the module doesn't

A lesson that quotes a specific law shows the current text of that citation every time you open it, even though the lesson's own wording was fixed when the module was published. The module's structure is stable; the law it points to is read fresh. If a citation hasn't been loaded anywhere on the platform yet, the lesson still opens — it simply shows the lesson's own wording without that piece attached, rather than failing to load at all.

A module also doesn't decide what's relevant to you by itself. Each module lists the kinds of things it's a good match for, and your case's own record — what's actually been found or flagged on it — is checked against that list. A module only shows up as suggested when something on your case genuinely matches what it addresses. Nothing about browsing the catalog or reading a suggested module changes what's already on your case; the suggestion is read-only, a pointer to something worth reading, not an action taken on your behalf.

Keeping module content immutable and assignments pinned to a version are the same idea applied twice: once to the content, once to what a person is actively working through. A publisher can improve or correct a module at any time by publishing a new version, without disturbing a single person who is partway through an older one. And once you're partway through a module, nothing changes the ground under your feet until you're the one who opens something new.
