Evidence and <span className="kw">findings</span> lives on three screens. The Records Index holds every file and record on your case. Case Detail is where the analysis of those records shows up — findings, patterns, and the discovery checklist sit here alongside your docket and timeline. Reports turns both into a document you can read straight through or hand to someone else. This page explains what each screen shows and how they connect.

## How it works

Vero's own premise is simple: you do not sort your evidence before it goes in. You add what you have, and Vero works out what it means. A text message that looks routine on its own can matter once it is read next to a dozen others like it. A bank statement you almost skipped can be the one that does not match the rest. Nothing here asks you to decide what is worth keeping before it goes on the case.

Once something is added, Vero reads it, tags it, and connects it to the rest of the case — the docket event it relates to, the party it involves, the other records it matches or contradicts. A finding is Vero's own analysis of what a group of records shows, written as a plain sentence, with every record it drew from listed next to it, so you can check the source yourself rather than take the sentence on faith.

Vero shows the same analysis to everyone connected to the case. If you are represented, your attorney sees the same findings and the same discovery checklist you do — there is no version with less shown. If you are self-represented, you see and decide on the same items an attorney would, including which ones need a closer, human look before anyone relies on them.

## What you can do here

- **Add a record.** A note, a photo, a voice memo, a message export, or a call recording — add it once, and Vero handles the rest.
- **Store and open your documents.** Court filings, financial records, and anything else you have as a file live in the Records Index, open to read whenever you need them.
- **Find a record by the event it belongs to.** Browse by the hearing or filing a record relates to, instead of scrolling a long list.
- **See what the analysis found.** Findings lay out what Vero noticed in your records, in plain sentences, with the source attached to each one.
- **See the patterns in your case.** Related findings group into patterns — repeated conduct, not a single incident — summarized in plain language.
- **Check what is still owed for discovery.** A simple checklist shows what is still needed from you and from the other side.
- **Review your call recordings.** Confirm who is speaking in each recording so the analysis built from it stays accurate.
- **See measured trends in your communications.** Where calls have been reviewed, Vero shows the trend gently, and only when you want to see it.
- **Generate a report.** Turn your records and findings into a report you can read straight through — evidence over time, messages over time, or a timeline.

## Who sees what

Case relationship decides what a person sees here, not case type. A represented party sees the same records, findings, and discovery checklist their attorney sees. A self-represented party sees and manages the same information directly, including deciding which findings need a closer look — there is no reduced view for either one.

## Availability

Pattern analysis, discovery completeness, and the communication measurements shown for call recordings depend on what your case has been set up with. If something described here is not on your screen, your case has not been enabled for that capability yet — that is a fact about your case's setup, not a mistake.

</Note>
