A call recording only helps your case if Vero has the speakers right — who said which part matters as much as what was said. Confirming a label takes one selection once you open the recording.

## Before you begin

Vero labels each voice in a recording automatically after it is added. You confirm or correct those labels; you do not need to label a recording from scratch.

1. Open the call recording on your case.
2. Review the speaker labels Vero assigned to each segment.
3. Select **Confirm** next to a label that is correct, or select it to reassign it to the right person.
4. Repeat for any other segment that needs a correction.
5. Select **Save**.

## What happens next

Once labels are confirmed, any finding or pattern built from the recording uses the corrected speaker for every quote it draws from. An unconfirmed recording is still analyzed, but Vero keeps learning the speakers as more recordings come in — confirming a label speeds that up and is not something you need to do for every recording.

Confirming a label on one recording also helps the ones that come after it. Once you have confirmed a voice a few times, Vero recognizes that same voice faster in new recordings, so the amount of correcting you need to do usually goes down over time rather than staying the same. You will still see the occasional label that needs a fix, especially the first time a new voice appears — a new opposing party, a new witness, a child old enough to be part of a call — but the pattern of most speakers being labeled correctly from the start holds once the voices on your case are established.

If a segment has more than one voice at once, or the audio is too unclear for Vero to separate the speakers, the segment is marked as unclear rather than guessed at. You can still confirm what you can make out and leave the rest unclear; a finding built from that segment notes that part of the source was not confidently attributed, so the uncertainty travels with the finding instead of being hidden inside it.
