Finding your way around a case's <span className="kw">corpus</span> takes only a few minutes once the case itself is set up. This guide walks you through reviewing documents, browsing findings and discovery status, and generating a report.

## What you'll learn

- How to review documents in a case's evidence corpus
- How to browse documents by the court event they relate to
- How to work the findings matrix and discovery completeness
- How to generate a report from a case's records and findings

## Before you begin

You need a case on your caseload before there's anything to review here. If you haven't added a case yet, see [Get started with your case](/your-case/attorneys/get-started) first.

### Step 1: Open a case's documents

From Case Detail, open Documents. Everything the firm or the client has uploaded appears here, each with a visibility state — standard, hidden, or sealed — and a category.

### Step 2: Browse by court event

Filter Documents by the hearing or filing an item relates to, instead of scrolling the full list. This is the fastest way to find what was produced around a specific docket entry.

### Step 3: Work the findings matrix

Open findings on Case Detail. Each finding carries the records it was drawn from, its attribution, any defenses raised against it, and a confidence score. Items that need a closer, human look sit in the review queue — decide them there rather than relying on the confidence score alone.

### Step 4: Check discovery completeness

Open the discovery checklist. Select a request set and review each item's status — produced, outstanding, or objected — against what the request actually required.

### Step 5: Generate a report

Go to Reports and choose what you need: evidence over time, messages over time, or a timeline. Generate it for the single case in front of you, or across your caseload if the report supports it.

## Tips

- Review patterns alongside individual findings — a pattern groups related findings as repeated conduct, and it can change how you read a single finding on its own.
- A client's flag arrives with its context already bundled. Read the flag before you clear it; it starts the review, not the other way around.
