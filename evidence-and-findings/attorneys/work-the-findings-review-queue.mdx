A finding is only as good as the review it's had. The <span className="kw">queue</span> is where a finding gets that review before it's treated as settled fact on the case.

1. **Open findings on Case Detail.** Every finding for the case appears here, most recent first.
2. **Filter by confidence or type.** Narrow the list to low-confidence findings or a specific claim type before you start, rather than working the full matrix in order.
3. **Open a finding and read its attribution.** Each finding names who or what it's attributed to, and lists every record it was drawn from — open the source records rather than taking the summary sentence on its own.
4. **Read the defenses attached to it, if any.** A finding can carry a defense the other side has raised against it; read it before deciding the finding stands as written.
5. **Decide the item.** Confirm the finding, flag it for further review, or dismiss it with a note explaining why. Every decision is attributed to the person who made it.
6. **Check the queue again after new records arrive.** A new record can change a finding's confidence or add a defense that wasn't there before, so a decided item is worth a second look if the underlying records change.

## Before you begin

Findings and pattern analysis are Premium Professional capabilities enabled per case. If findings don't appear on a case, the case hasn't been enabled for the capability yet.

## What happens next

A decided finding keeps its decision and the name of who made it, visible to anyone with access to the case, including a represented client. On a self-represented case, the party in the case decides these items directly rather than routing them through a review queue on your side.
