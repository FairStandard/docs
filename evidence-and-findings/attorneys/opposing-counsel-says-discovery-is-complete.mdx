Opposing counsel has sent a letter, or said on a call, that their client's discovery production is complete. Your own checklist tells a different story, and you need to know exactly where the gap is before you respond, rather than pointing to a general sense that something is missing.

## What to do

Open the discovery checklist for the case and select the request set the other side says is closed. Go through each item's status one at a time: produced, outstanding, or <span className="kw">objected</span>. An item marked objected is not the same problem as one marked outstanding — an objection needs a response addressing the stated ground, while an outstanding item may just need a follow-up demand. Cross-check what's marked produced against the records actually attached to the case; if a request calls for a category of document and only part of it has arrived, the checklist will show it as produced only for the items actually logged, not for the request as a whole. Where findings or patterns touch the same subject matter as an outstanding request, review them too — a pattern built from what has arrived can point directly at what a still-outstanding item would need to contain.

## What Vero does for you

The checklist tracks what has been requested against what has actually come in and been logged for each request set, so it reflects the current state of the exchange rather than either side's account of it. It does not tell you whether a stated objection is valid, or draft the response you'd send if a genuine gap exists — those are judgment calls that depend on facts the checklist can't capture on its own, like whether a deadline was extended by agreement or whether a request was overbroad to begin with. Where the case is represented, your client sees the same checklist and the same statuses you do.
