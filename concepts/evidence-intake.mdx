Most people approach evidence the wrong way — they try to curate before they upload, holding back items they think are irrelevant or too messy to use. FairStandard is built around the opposite principle: give the engine everything you have, and let it find what matters. A text message you dismissed as unimportant may establish a pattern. A bank statement you thought was routine may contain the discrepancy that changes the case. Upload first. The engine decides what is signal.

## What You Can Upload

FairStandard accepts a broad range of file types across every category of evidence that appears in family law and related proceedings:

- **Audio and video recordings** — MP3, MP4, M4A
- **Text and message exports** — iMessage backups, WhatsApp exports, SMS backup files
- **Court orders and filings** — PDF, DOCX
- **Financial documents** — bank statements, tax returns, pay stubs (PDF, XLSX)
- **Photos and screenshots** — PNG, JPG
- **Opposing party filings** — any document served on you or filed by the other side

If you have it and it relates to your case, upload it.

## What the Engine Finds

Once your evidence is in, the engine gets to work. It does not simply store files — it reads them, cross-references them, and surfaces what matters:

- **Patterns of behavior** across communications and recordings — repeated conduct that establishes a course of action rather than an isolated incident
- **Financial discrepancies and hidden assets** — inconsistencies between declared income, lifestyle evidence, and transaction records
- **Documents referenced in discovery that were never produced** — gaps between what was requested and what was provided
- **Specific provisions of court orders that were violated** — the engine maps conduct against the order's exact language and flags deviations
- **All findings cited back to the source material** — every flag includes the document, timestamp, or record it came from, so the citation is ready to use

<Warning>
  Do not upload documents that are subject to a court-ordered protective order
  without consulting your attorney first.
</Warning>

## How Evidence Is Organized

You do not need to organize your uploads manually. When a file lands in your Evidence Vault, the engine categorizes it by type, tags it with relevant parties, dates, and topics, and links it to the applicable statutes and timeline events in your case. A recording from a disputed custody exchange is automatically tied to the relevant order provisions and the date on your case timeline. A bank statement is cross-referenced against other financial records in the vault.

Nothing is lost in a folder. Every item is findable by date, type, party, or the legal issue it bears on. When you are preparing a filing or heading into a hearing, the evidence you need is already organized and cited — you are not searching through downloads at midnight before a court date.
