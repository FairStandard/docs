A single Texas custody case touches more than 7,000 statute sections spread across six codes and rule sets — the Family Code, the Civil Practice and Remedies Code, the Government Code, the Texas Rules of Civil Procedure, the Texas Rules of Evidence, and applicable local rules. No litigant is expected to know all of that. No single attorney holds it all in active memory for every case type in every state they practice. Vero's Law Assembly Engine solves this problem at the moment you create a case, before you file a single document.

## How it works

When you create a case and select your state and case type, the engine immediately identifies every applicable statute section, rule set, and controlling precedent for your specific situation. It does not hand you the entire state code — it surfaces only the provisions that govern your case type and jurisdiction. The result is a curated, cited body of law that is specific to what you are facing.

As you add evidence and build out your case facts, the engine refines what it surfaces. A custody dispute that involves relocation draws on a different set of provisions than one that does not. A case with documented domestic violence triggers additional statutes. The assembly adapts to your facts, not a generic template.

Every assembled provision is browsable and linked. Follow a citation from a draft filing back to the governing statute. Follow a statute forward to the evidence and filings where it appears. Nothing floats in isolation.

<Tip>
  You do not need to know which laws apply before creating a case. Select your
  state and case type — Vero handles the rest.
</Tip>

## What the engine surfaces

- **Applicable statutes by code and rule set** — every section that governs your case type, organized by source
- **Controlling case law and doctrine** — the precedents that courts in your jurisdiction apply when deciding cases like yours
- **Procedural rules for your jurisdiction** — filing deadlines, required forms, service requirements, and local court rules
- **Specific provisions relevant to your facts** — sections filtered by the details you have entered and the evidence you have uploaded

## Why this matters

The disparity in legal outcomes has never been purely about whether someone had an attorney. It has also been about whether anyone — attorney or litigant — had the full picture. A busy solo practitioner handling fifty family law cases cannot carry 7,000 statute sections in active recall for every client. A self-represented litigant has no realistic path to assembling that picture manually.

The Law Assembly Engine makes the complete legal framework available to every case on the platform, regardless of who is running it. An attorney using Vero walks into every hearing with the full statutory picture assembled and cited. A self-represented litigant using Vero has the same foundation. That is what equal access to the law looks like in practice.
